A blocked card at the checkout is a customer who calls support, or leaves. A missed fraud is a chargeback. The hard cases sit in the middle, where the data alone can't tell, and the one person who knows the answer is never asked.

Impact

Fewer frozen cards. Less fraud. Customers asked only when it matters.

Most fraud systems have two answers for an unclear payment: decline it and upset a genuine customer, or approve it and risk a loss. Subpoena.ai adds a third: ask the cardholder, safely, and decide with their answer.

For cardholders

  • Genuine purchases go through. An unusual but real payment is confirmed with one reply instead of a decline at checkout.
  • No call-centre queue. The question arrives on WhatsApp, SMS or a phone call, in the app they already use, with nothing to install.
  • Protected from takeovers. If their SIM was just swapped, nobody is asked: the payment is blocked and sent to an analyst.
  • A clear reason. Every decision comes with a plain-English explanation the bank can share.

For fraud teams

  • Only the hard cases reach a person. Clear cases are decided automatically; deadlocks are resolved by the cardholder.
  • Every decision shows its evidence. The full debate, the cited findings and the testimony are in the Console.
  • Humans stay in charge. Analysts can override any verdict, and every change is in the audit log.

For the bank

  • Revenue kept. Fewer false declines means fewer abandoned purchases and fewer customers who switch cards.
  • Losses avoided. Mule rings, sanctioned merchants and SIM-swap takeovers are blocked before money moves.
  • Its own risk appetite. A versioned policy, tested in a simulator before it's published, turns the model's probability into the decision.

How it works

From payment to verdict, in four steps.

1

Screen

Hard rules and a risk score clear the obvious cases in under a millisecond. Only the unclear ones go to court.

2

Debate

The Clerk and five expert agents gather evidence. Prosecutor and Defense argue; any claim without a cited finding is struck before the Judge sees it.

3

Subpoena

On a deadlock, the Bailiff checks for a SIM swap and the cardholder gets one question and a one-time code by WhatsApp, SMS or a phone call.

4

Verdict

The Judge re-rules with the verified testimony. The bank gets a signed webhook; the Stenographer writes the plain-English why.

The courtroom

Every agent has one job, and only the tools for it.

Each agent connects to its own MCP endpoint, so it can only reach what its role needs. The Prosecutor can't message a customer; the Witness can't see the evidence.

Clerk

Opens the case and pulls baselines, history and device data.

Expert witnesses

Geo, Device, Velocity, Merchant and Graph each file cited findings.

Prosecutor

Argues for blocking, from the findings only.

Defense

Argues for approving, and rebuts the prosecution.

Judge

Rules a calibrated p_fraud and, if needed, issues the subpoena.

Bailiff

SIM-swap lookup and one-time code before any testimony counts.

Stenographer

Explains the verdict in plain English for analysts and customers.

Analyst

The human appeals court: override any verdict from the Console.

The witness

Ask the customer where they already are.

The subpoena goes out on the first channel the bank's policy allows. If it isn't delivered, because the WhatsApp window has closed or a call isn't answered, it moves to the next.

Built for a regulated room

Guardrails are in the architecture, not the prompt.

Testimony is untrusted

A tool-less extractor turns the customer's words into structured claims. "Ignore previous instructions" is just text.

Identity before testimony

Carrier SIM-swap check, then a one-time code. Unknown SIM status fails closed.

Cite or be struck

Arguments that don't reference a finding never reach the Judge.

Policy decides, not the model

The Judge outputs a probability; the bank's versioned policy maps it to an outcome.

Every change is on record

Append-only audit log, analyst overrides with reasons, signed webhooks.

Least privilege for people

Google sign-in, Admin / Analyst / Viewer roles, personal data masked for viewers.

Who it's for

Built for anyone who has to say yes or no to a payment.

Card issuers and banks

Card payments and wires in the grey zone, where both mistakes are costly: declining a genuine customer or letting fraud through.

Neobanks and wallets

Customers who already live in chat apps, and a small fraud team that can't review every alert.

Payment processors

One API in front of many merchants, with a separate workspace, policy and audit log for each client.

How a bank uses it

  1. 1
    Connect

    Create an API key in the Console and send each payment to POST /v1/evaluations, with the cardholder's phone if they can be asked.

  2. 2
    Set the policy

    Choose the approve and block thresholds, the minimum amount worth a subpoena and the channels allowed, then replay past cases in the simulator before publishing.

  3. 3
    Receive decisions

    Clear cases come back in the response. Courtroom cases post a signed webhook, or go to Slack, Pub/Sub or BigQuery.

  4. 4
    Work the queue

    Analysts watch cases live, read the transcript, review escalations and override with a reason. The Overview shows the customer impact.
